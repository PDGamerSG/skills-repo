# Evidence-led Research

Produce a sourced answer that distinguishes what was found, what follows from it, and what remains unknown. This skill uses available browsing and public database access; no paid research service or API key is required for the core workflow.

## Work from the question

Infer the scope from the conversation and relevant project files. Clarify only missing information that would materially change the investigation, while continuing independent work. For an open field, compare mechanisms across several engineering or scientific domains rather than choosing a fashionable label. Preserve the project's earlier rejected ideas and evaluation rules.

## Retrieve and inspect

- Search concepts, mechanisms, synonyms, failure modes, and older-field equivalents. Use both patents and non-patent literature when invention discovery is requested.
- Prefer original papers, standards, official technical documentation, patent claims, and institutional disclosures. Search snippets and secondary summaries are leads until the underlying source is read.
- Record exact queries, search date, source/database, documents opened, source locations, and retrieval failures in a search log. Distinguish a bounded public-source screen from a systematic or exhaustive review.
- Verify title, identifier, authors/assignee, publication date, and the passage supporting each material claim. Distinguish first public disclosure from later patent grant or revision dates.
- Search for contrary evidence and the closest competing mechanism. Repeated summaries of one study are not independent evidence.
- Treat inaccessible documents as unresolved leads. A failed search or empty result is not evidence that a mechanism is new.

## Evaluate and deliver

Build an evidence table with claim, primary source link, supporting location, observed teaching, limitations, and whether the claim is a fact, inference, or proposal. Identify engineering feasibility separately from prior-art novelty. Synthetic examples, simulations, and fitted models do not establish novelty or empirical performance.

Deliver the concrete conclusion first, then the important evidence, uncertainties, and the next falsifying check. Save longer reports and logs in the user's project when useful. Use available native document tools for requested exports. Optional tools mentioned in community skills are alternatives, not a requirement to install additional services or transmit private material.
